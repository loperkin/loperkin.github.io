Build an ESPHome MultiSensor for Home Assistant

Instead of scattering separate sensors around a room, this open-source project combines mmWave presence detection, ambient light, temperature and humidity in one ESP32-C3 Super Mini-based device. The original design can be assembled on a breadboard, or built using a custom PCB and a 3D-printed enclosure.

Use the readings together to automate lighting when a room is dark and occupied, keep lights on while someone is sitting still, monitor comfort, and track room-level occupancy.

Components used in the original design

  • ESP32-C3 Super Mini: microcontroller running ESPHome
  • HLK-LD2410C: mmWave presence sensor
  • BH1750: ambient light sensor
  • DHT22: temperature and humidity sensor
  • Breadboard and jumper wires: convenient for prototyping
  • Optional: custom PCB and printable enclosure

Install and configure ESPHome

  1. Review the firmware folder and adapt the ESPHome configuration to your own hardware, network and Home Assistant installation.
  2. Connect the ESP32-C3 Super Mini by USB and flash the appropriate configuration.
  3. Verify presence, light, temperature and humidity readings before putting the electronics into an enclosure.
  4. Explore the Home Assistant automation examples and adapt them to your room and devices.

If the ESP32-C3 will not enter flashing mode

The project documents a recovery sequence for boards that repeatedly reset: hold BOOT, press and release RESET while holding BOOT, then release BOOT and retry flashing over USB. This is a troubleshooting step, not something every board requires.

Home Assistant automation ideas

Combine presence detected with low ambient light to turn on a room light only when needed. Keep it on while someone is still present, then turn it off when the room becomes unoccupied. You can also use room temperature and occupancy to inform HVAC automations.

3D-print the original enclosure

The repository includes STL files for the original MultiSensor enclosure. Download the parts, inspect them in your slicer, and test-fit your PCB and sensors before final assembly. PLA or PETG, a 0.20 mm layer height and 15–20% infill are suggested starting points in the project documentation—not validated settings for every STL.

Download the enclosure STL files. The separate Decora MultiSensor uses a different form factor and may need different printed parts.
