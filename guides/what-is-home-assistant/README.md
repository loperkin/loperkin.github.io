## What is Home Assistant?

Home Assistant is free, open-source home automation software that brings compatible smart devices into one place. It lets you control devices and create automations that coordinate them, even when they come from different manufacturers. Think of it as a universal remote for your home—but one that can also follow rules you set.

## Why do smart homes need something like this?

Remember when a TV, VCR, DVD player and stereo receiver each had their own remote? Modern smart homes can have a similar problem: lights in one app, a thermostat in another, cameras in a third. These separate collections of products are often called ecosystems. Some work together, but many have limitations. Home Assistant supports integrations that help bridge compatible products and services.

## What can Home Assistant do?

You can put compatible lights, switches, sensors, thermostats, locks and other devices on dashboards. More importantly, you can create automations: turn on hallway lights when motion is detected, adjust heating or cooling when everyone leaves, or turn off selected lights at bedtime. Those actions depend on the devices, connections and automations you configure.

## Does it work with every smart device?

No. Home Assistant supports a large and growing collection of integrations, but not every product, feature or cloud service works with it. Some integrations are local; others require the internet or a manufacturer's account. Always check compatibility for the exact model.

## Is Home Assistant private and local?

Home Assistant emphasizes local control and privacy. Many devices can continue working locally without internet access, provided your home network, Home Assistant system and relevant devices still have power. Cloud-dependent integrations and remote services may stop working during an internet outage.

## Do you need to be a programmer?

No programming is required for many common tasks, although learning a few concepts helps. Home Assistant is more hands-on than many single-brand smart-home apps. Start with one compatible device and one simple automation before building a whole-house system.
